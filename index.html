The festival

Concept and story

Wiñoy Flor is a fictional ten-day festival that celebrates the rebirth of nature. Its name blends Wiñoy (“return” in Mapudungun, as in Wiñoy Tripantu, the return of the sun) with Flor, the symbol of Medellín and of the Feria de las Flores. Its motto is “When the sun returns, the flowers bloom.”

The festival takes place simultaneously at two venues: Medellín (Colombia) and Temuco (Chile). Each venue lives its own activities, but the two connect live at the most important moments: the New Fire ceremony, the storytelling night and the grand final parade. This way, a paisa silletero and a Mapuche family can “meet” through a screen and share the same sunrise.

The big idea is to fuse two symbols: the silleta (the flower disc carried by the silleteros) and the kultrún (the Mapuche ceremonial drum, which represents the universe). At Wiñoy Flor, silletas are decorated with Mapuche motifs and kultrunes are adorned with flowers. The final parade is called the Grand Parade of Silleteros and Weichafes of the Sun.

The legend of the festival (an invented story)

They say that on the longest night of the year, when the sun had almost fallen asleep, Ñuke Mapu (Mother Earth) came down to the mountain of Santa Elena and found a silletero carrying a disc of flowers on his back. She told him: “Your flowers keep the warmth of the sun; my fires keep its memory.” Together they lit the New Fire, and from its sparks Pétalo was born: a flower that never wilts and travels every year between Colombia and Chile to announce that the sun is coming back. That is why, every June 21, both peoples light the fire at the same time.

Audience

Families, national and international tourists, Indigenous and peasant communities, young people and students.

Entry

Free for ceremonies and parades; workshops and concerts by registration.

Why these dates?

June 21 is the date of We Tripantu (the winter solstice in the Southern Hemisphere), which is why it is the opening day. The ten-day length and the closing with the silleteros parade come from the Feria de las Flores in Medellín, which lasts about ten days and ends with its great Silleteros Parade (in 2026, from July 31 to August 9, with the parade on the closing Sunday). It is a design choice: Medellín’s fair takes place in August, but here it is moved to the solstice to join it with We Tripantu.

Wiñoy Flor is a fictional festival created from real celebrations: the Feria de las Flores (Colombia) and We Tripantu (Chile). Its dates, places and menus are design proposals, not real data.

Guest dishes and drinks from Colombia

Our partners in Colombia shared real facts about their traditional food and drinks. They join the Gastronomic Route (day 8) as guests. The cultural facts are real; the way they are used in the festival is a design proposal.

Sancocho de gallina

Real fact

A typical dish of Cali and the Valle del Cauca. It is made with hen, green plantain, yuca and corn on the cob, among other ingredients; recipes vary.

In Wiñoy Flor

Guest dish at the Gastronomic Route (day 8), next to the Sancocho Wiñoy (lamb and pine nut). It lets you compare two versions of sancocho: the one from the Valle del Cauca and the one from Wiñoy Flor.

Guarapo

Real fact

A sweet, refreshing drink made from freshly squeezed sugarcane juice. It is served cold with ice and lime, and sometimes left to ferment slightly, which gives it a stronger, more traditional flavor. It is very popular in rural areas and at local festivals.

In Wiñoy Flor

New drink “Guarapo Wiñoy” at the Gastronomic Route (day 8): cold with lime in Medellín, warm with lime and cinnamon in Temuco. The fresh version is alcohol-free; the slightly fermented one is for adults only, following the same logic as the two versions of muday.

Lulada

Real fact

A very cold, sweet drink made with lulo, lime, sugar and water. The lulo pulp is chopped by hand.

In Wiñoy Flor

The Silleta Fresca already has lulo: it can be prepared lulada-style, with hand-chopped pulp, lime and maqui.

Champús

Real fact

A drink from the Valle del Cauca with cooked corn, lulo, pineapple and panela; every family has its own recipe.

In Wiñoy Flor

Guest drink at the Gastronomic Route (day 8). It shares corn and panela with the Muday with panela.

A note about muday

Traditional Mapuche muday is a fermented grain drink (wheat, corn and others) offered in ceremonies such as the nguillatún. That is why Wiñoy Flor has both versions: the fermented one, faithful to tradition, and the unfermented one. See the justification in the Respect section.

People and traditions

Characters, music, dance and clothing

Silletero / Silletera Wiñoy

Carries a silleta decorated with Mapuche symbols and leads the parade.

Guardian of the New Fire

A woman with a kultrún and a trarilonko; she guides the dawn ceremony and lights the fire.

Pétalo, the mascot

The official mascot, a smiling flower born from the New Fire. It appears on posters, games and children’s workshops.

Music

The music mixes the instruments of both peoples: the tiple and the guitar of paisa trova with the Mapuche kultrún, trutruka and pifilka. Ül singing joins the paisa coplas.

Dance

The main dance is the Dance of the New Sun: a circle of dancers, inspired by the Mapuche purrún, that turns around the fire while the silleteros advance to the rhythm of the music. Everyone can join.

Clothing

Silleteros wear the aguadeño hat and a paisa ruana woven with Mapuche motifs. Mapuche women wear the silver trarilonko adorned with flowers. Silletas carry kultrún patterns and the colors of both flags.

Ruana

Real fact

An Andean garment of thick, handwoven sheep’s wool that protects from the cold of the mountains. It is an important symbol of Colombian peasant and Andean culture.

In Wiñoy Flor

It is already part of the festival’s clothing (“paisa ruana woven with Mapuche motifs”). Now its origin and its use as a warm layer are explained, which is very useful in Temuco’s winter (~3–12 °C).

Feria de Cali

Real fact

It is held from December 25 to 30. It starts with the Salsódromo, a huge parade of dancers, and fills the city with salsa and rumba.

In Wiñoy Flor

Cali is not a venue: its fair is in December and Wiñoy Flor is in June. It takes part as a symbolic guest city at the fusion concerts (day 6).

Salsa and Grupo Niche

Real fact

Salsa is the emblematic music of Cali. “Cali Pachanguero”, by Grupo Niche, has been the official song of the Feria de Cali since 1984.

In Wiñoy Flor

Day 6: a “Salsa and kultrún night”, where Colombian salsa joins the kultrún and the trutruka. Salsa is already among the genres at the Feria de las Flores concerts.

The ruana and the poncho: similar garments, but different

The ruana and the poncho are very similar woolen garments, but they are not the same. According to the sources consulted, the ruana is an Andean garment of thick sheep’s wool, handwoven and open at the front, while the poncho is characterized by an opening to pass the head through. Names also change by region: in Antioquia a ruana is sometimes called a “poncho”. We found no single official definition, so the festival treats each garment separately. At Wiñoy Flor the word ruana is used for the paisa garment, with Mapuche designs, and it is not mixed with the poncho. It keeps you warm especially in Temuco’s winter.

Cultural respect

Why the fusion makes sense

This section explains why the most delicate decisions of the festival make sense within both traditions. When a fact comes from a source, it is said; when we did not find a reliable source, it is described as a design proposal.

7.1 Joining a ceremony with a festive parade

We Tripantu is not only an intimate act: it is described as the Mapuche New Year and the return of the sun, with a rogativa at dawn, and today it is also celebrated in public spaces organized by institutions and Indigenous communities during solstice week. The Feria de las Flores, for its part, is a public parade that closes with the silleteros.

Wiñoy Flor does not mix both moments into one: the dawn ceremony (day 1) is led by the host Mapuche communities, and the festive parade takes place during the day (day 10). The two symbols, the silleta and the kultrún, are combined visually, without altering the meaning of the ceremony.

7.2 The name “Weichafes del Sol”

Weichafe means “warrior” in Mapudungun, and weichan is the struggle. Beyond its military sense, the weichafe has a ritual role: in communities such as Lumaco and Traiguén, weichafes represent in the nguillatún the weichan, or fight of the winds before the harvest, and are considered protectors of the land.

Calling those who accompany the parade “Weichafes of the Sun” is justified this way: they are the guardians who protect the return of the sun, just as the weichafe protects their community.

Clarification: today the term is also used in political contexts (for example, the name of an armed group), so the festival uses it only in its ceremonial and cultural sense, and the use of the name should be agreed with the host communities.

7.3 Drinks with alcohol

Muday. Sources describe muday as a traditional Mapuche drink fermented from grains (wheat, corn and others), low in alcohol (around 1–3% depending on the recipe), offered at the nguillatún. At a ceremony at Universidad Austral de Chile, mudai (wheat chicha) was placed with seeds under a canelo tree during the rogativa to the east. That is why the festival includes the fermented muday, faithful to tradition, and an unfermented version for children and people who do not drink alcohol.

Fuego Nuevo. It mixes Antioquian aguardiente (a traditional paisa drink) with apple chicha from southern Chile. We did not find a reliable source linking apple chicha to We Tripantu, so it is a design proposal: a festive toast that unites one drink from each country, served warm for Temuco’s cold weather. An alcohol-free alternative exists (Silleta Fresca).

7.4 What is invented and what is not

The legend, Pétalo, the Dance of the New Sun and the fusion dishes are new creations. The Mapuche symbols (kultrún, trarilonko, purrún) and the paisa ones (silleta, ruana, aguadeño hat) are used as real elements of each culture and are represented faithfully and respectfully.

7.5 Guest contributions from Colombia: what does not change
  • Dates and venues: the festival is still June 21–30 in Medellín and Temuco. The Feria de Cali takes place in December, so Cali only takes part as a guest city.
  • The ceremony: the New Fire (day 1) and the rogativa are led by the host Mapuche communities, as explained in 7.1. There is no salsa, guarapo or guest sancocho there: the contributions are used only in festive activities (days 6 and 8).
  • Alcohol: slightly fermented guarapo is for adults only and always has an alcohol-free alternative, like muday and Fuego Nuevo.
  • Real and invented: the cultural facts shared by our partners are real. “Guarapo Wiñoy”, the “Salsa and kultrún night” and Cali as a guest city are new creations of the festival.
Caring for the planet

Sustainable practices

Wiñoy Flor has one main sustainable practice and several complementary ones. All of them are design proposals: they rely on real problems and programs, which are cited with their sources, but we do not claim that they already exist at the original festivals.

Main practice

Flowers that return to the earth

What it is. After the Grand Parade of Silleteros and Weichafes of the Sun, and at the end of every activity with flowers, no flower is thrown away. Flowers and foliage from the silletas are sorted by condition: fresh ones are given to families, care homes and host communities; those that can be dried are reused in craft workshops; and the rest goes to composting to become fertilizer.

How it works. Each venue has an “Earth Point” (Punto de la Tierra), a drop-off zone run by volunteers, silleteros and communities. Silleteros deliver their dismantled silletas there. The resulting compost goes back to the silletero families of Santa Elena, the Botanical Garden and the gardens of the community ruka in Temuco, so that the land that gave the flowers gets something in return.

Why it helps. Flowers are organic waste: if they end up in a landfill they decompose without being used, while composting turns them into fertilizer and reduces the festival’s trash. The practice builds on capacities that already exist: the Mayor’s Office of Medellín composts organic waste in its waste management programs, and the city’s cleaning company prepares a special operation after the Silleteros Parade to leave the streets clean. We did not find a source explaining what is done today with the flowers of the silletas, so this part is a proposal.

Cultural meaning. In We Tripantu, the renewal of the cycle is understood as a commitment to care for Ñuke Mapu, Mother Earth. Returning the flowers to the earth unites both cultures: the silletero hands over the flower he grew and the earth gives it back as fertilizer, just as in the festival’s legend, where Ñuke Mapu and the silletero light the New Fire together.

Complementary practice

Water guardians

What it is. Before the festival, communities, volunteers and students from both countries clean and care for a nearby water source at each venue: the Santa Elena creek, which crosses Medellín, and the bank of the Cautín River and its wetlands in Temuco. The days open with a short greeting to the water, led by the Mapuche communities and the silletero families.

How it works. The days are held together with what already exists, not in place of it. In Medellín, the Mayor’s Office has already worked on the Santa Elena creek and maintains the city’s creeks. In Temuco, neighborhood organizations have drawn attention to the trash on the Cautín bank, the municipality reports cleanup operations, and volunteer days have already been held in wetlands. The festival would join these efforts and make them visible, without promising to clean an entire river.

Why it helps and what it means. Water is central to We Tripantu: many families bathe in rivers or streams at dawn to renew themselves, and several sources link this to the commitment to care for nature. Caring for the water before the festival makes that ritual gesture happen in cared-for water, and also protects the surroundings that tourists visit.

Complementary practice

Live simultaneous festival

What it is. Because Wiñoy Flor takes place at the same time in Medellín and Temuco, nobody needs to cross the continent to share the New Fire ceremony, the storytelling night or the grand parade: each person lives the festival in their own city and meets the other venue through screens.

Why it helps. Studies on virtual and hybrid events show that replacing long trips greatly reduces an event’s emissions, although the energy used by streaming and screen fatigue should be taken into account. That is why the festival remains in person at each venue and the live link is used only at key moments.

The practices reinforce each other: flowers return to the earth, the water that irrigates that earth is cared for, and the live link avoids unnecessary trips.
